2. Improve avalanche and avalanche riding
- Fix bug where blocks sometimes sprout from opposite of avalanche sweep direction by assigning tile sprouts a partial order by the time they were generated by the avalanche mass ball (ties broken by distance along the mass ball's direction of movement). ask that tiles never initiate generation by a parent lower in the ordering
- I'd really like it to feel like the player can ride avalanches, and jump from them using momentum. it seems like doing this will require some hacks. the basic idea should be that anywhere near moving blocks, the player can hook into the mass flow and follow it, either staying on top of, or following, the mass creation front. This is tricky, because there's no actual mass flow in the avalanche, it's actual a front of new mass spawning. But the behavior we're looking for is more like if earth was actually erupting from the ground, and being pushed up continuously by the earth beneath, so that, e.g., if the player attached themselves to the ground at the start of the eruption, they'd end up at the peak of the eruption in the end. If we had a perfect simulation of reality, we would have a vector field of the mass flow rate at each point in the world. We would then allow the player to hook into this mass anywhere, treating any moving point within the mass as an attachment point analagous to a moving surface for the standing movement state. The player would then be carried along naturally, and would be able to move around naturally within the front. For instance, if they held right, this would tell the game to move the player towards the right in the reference frame of their current attachement point, shifting their attachment point right as well. The really tricky part is actually building the vector field, in such a way that it matches what the blocks are doing while also keeping the player movement smooth and predictable, not dominated by tile jitter. One way to do it would be to use the local, distance weighted, average of tile growth, basically like we've alreaady been doing, but this is limited because tiles only grow in horizontal or vertical directions. It might be easier to look at the rate at which mass is appearing within tiles, not even paying attention to sprout direction, and use that to derive front speed and location.
